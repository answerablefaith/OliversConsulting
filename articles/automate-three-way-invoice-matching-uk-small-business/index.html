How to automate three-way invoice matching for a UK small business

Match authorised orders, actual receipts and supplier invoices, then give every discrepancy a clear route to resolution.

Published 26 October 2026Updated 26 October 2026By Henry Oliver
Quick answer

Automate three-way invoice matching by connecting purchase-order lines, confirmed goods receipts and supplier invoice lines using trustworthy references. Automatically pass transactions that agree under documented rules, and send short deliveries, price differences, missing receipts and possible duplicates to the right reviewer before an invoice is approved or posted. Start with matching features already available in the business's purchasing or accounting tools.

An invoice may match what was ordered perfectly and still be wrong to pay. The goods could be missing, only partly delivered or already invoiced. That is why the receipt record matters as much as the purchase order.

Three-way matching is the control between buying and paying. Done well, the repeatable comparisons run automatically and finance sees only the differences that need decisions.

Our view: do not speed up payment processing by treating an invoice as proof of delivery. Automate the comparison and make every missing piece of evidence visible.

What does three-way matching compare?

A three-way match checks three records that describe the same purchase from different perspectives. The purchase order records what was authorised, the goods receipt records what actually arrived, and the supplier invoice records what the supplier wants to be paid. Matching all three prevents a clean-looking invoice from bypassing a missing delivery or an unapproved price change. For services, an authorised service-entry or completion record can provide the receipt evidence.

The Olivers Consulting three-evidence matching gate

Order authorised → Receipt confirmed → Invoice agreed

All three evidence types must refer to the same supplier, order and relevant lines. A match moves forward only when quantity, unit price, currency and applicable charges are consistent with the approved policy. Missing or conflicting evidence becomes an exception—not an invented receipt.

How do you match the three records automatically?

Start with a valid supplier identifier and a purchase order number. Connect invoice lines to the relevant PO lines and receipt events. Compare ordered quantity, cumulative received quantity, previously invoiced quantity, unit of measure, agreed unit price, currency and any separately approved freight or other charges. Keep a stable key for each invoice line, order line and receipt transaction. A document reference alone is not enough if the same order can be billed in stages.

Do you need an exact match?

An exact match is the strongest candidate for automatic processing, but some businesses permit limited, documented tolerances for particular differences. Do not copy another organisation's percentage tolerances. Finance and procurement should set their own policy based on purchasing agreements, materiality and risk. A difference within tolerance may proceed under an approved rule; anything outside it needs a named reviewer. Never silently absorb differences into a balancing entry.

How should partial deliveries be handled?

Suppose a purchase order is for 100 units, 70 have been received, and the supplier invoices all 100. The system should recognise the received 70 against the original order but hold the remaining 30 for delivery evidence or an authorised exception. If the supplier invoices the first 70 now and the final 30 later, the matching process must account for previous invoices so the order cannot be paid twice. Distinguish partial receipt from a missing receipt or a disputed shortage.

What if a supplier sends a price change?

A revised invoice price does not itself amend the agreed purchase order. Record the difference against the affected line, route it to the purchaser and require an authorised price change or credit before releasing the line. If a change is approved, preserve the evidence of who authorised the variation. That gives staff an actionable exception instead of a vague “invoice does not match” message.

How should duplicate invoices be detected?

Before posting, check the supplier ID, invoice number and existing invoices, then examine suspicious combinations of amount, date and document content. The same invoice can arrive by email and supplier portal or be resent after a query. The workflow should recognise a retry and link it to the original record rather than create a fresh payable. Duplicate checks complement—but do not replace—line-level three-way matching.

What if no goods-receipt note exists?

Introduce a lightweight receiving step with order ID, line, actual quantity, receipt date and responsible receiver. For professional services, a service-completion or acceptance record may play the equivalent role. Do not simply mark goods received because the invoice arrived. How can it work without a full procurement system?

A UK SME can start with an existing accounting or purchasing application, a structured receiving log and a controlled invoice intake route. The key is consistent order identifiers, clear field ownership and an exception queue. Use existing product functionality where it covers the workflow; a targeted integration may be useful where receipts live in one system and invoices in another. How should exceptions be routed?

Send the discrepancy to the person able to resolve it. A missing receipt goes to the receiving team or service owner, a price variance to the buyer, a duplicate invoice to accounts payable and a supplier identity conflict to finance or supplier-data ownership. Include the relevant PO, invoice, receipt lines and precise mismatch. When a reviewer changes the result, record their decision and re-evaluate the match before posting. How is this different from invoice-processing automation?

Invoice processing starts with capture, extraction, validation and approval of an incoming bill. Three-way matching answers a narrower question: does the invoice agree with the authorised order and what was actually received? Worked example: one order, two receipts, one invoice

Consider a hypothetical distributor. It orders 50 units at £20 each. The first delivery contains 30 units and the second 20, each with its own receipt event. The supplier invoices 50 units at the authorised unit price. The workflow combines the two verified receipts, checks that none of the 50 units were already invoiced, and can record a complete match. If the second receipt is missing, the invoice remains partially unmatched despite the correct order total. This example illustrates the matching logic, not a client result.

What should you test in a pilot?

  1. Choose one supplier category with reliable purchase orders.
  2. Identify how goods or services are confirmed as received.
  3. Map supplier, PO, receipt and invoice line identifiers.
  4. Set explicit approval and tolerance rules with finance.
  5. Run historical exact matches and reconcile the outcome.
  6. Test partial deliveries, duplicates, price changes, missing receipts and credit notes.
  7. Compare automatic decisions with the existing manual process before enabling posting.

Measure accurately matched lines, unmatched lines, false matches, unresolved differences and time spent chasing evidence. A high automatic match rate is not useful if incorrect invoices slip through.

When is custom matching automation worth considering?

Use the purchasing or accounts system's built-in matching features first. Custom workflow components become attractive when PO lines, goods receipts and invoices live in different systems; when one order is invoiced in stages; or when exception investigation consumes disproportionate time. Questions about three-way invoice matching

What is three-way invoice matching?

Three-way matching compares the supplier invoice, purchase order and goods-receipt record to establish whether the invoiced items, quantities and prices agree with what the business ordered and received.

Can three-way matching be fully automated?

Unambiguous matches can proceed under documented rules and approval policies. Missing goods receipts, part deliveries, conflicting quantities and prices outside agreed tolerances should be held for review.

What if a small business has no goods-receipt system?

Use a lightweight receiving record linked to each purchase order, or a documented service-acceptance record for services. Without reliable evidence of receipt, a process is invoice-to-order matching rather than a true three-way match.
