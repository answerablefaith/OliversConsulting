How to automate sales-to-operations handover after a deal closes

Move approved scope, milestones and customer information from a closed deal into delivery—with an accountable acceptance gate.

Published 30 October 2026Updated 30 October 2026By Henry Oliver
Quick answer

Automate sales-to-operations handover by triggering a provisional delivery record when a deal is confirmed won, copying the approved scope and essential customer information, checking for missing delivery requirements and asking an accountable operations owner to accept the work. Create delivery tasks only after the handover passes its readiness checks. This removes rekeying without mistaking a sales win for operational readiness.

A salesperson can close a deal while the delivery team still knows almost nothing about what was promised. The project tool may get a new record, but that does not mean anyone has agreed the scope, dates or ownership.

Automating this handover well means transferring the evidence and the responsibility—not only the notification that a deal was won.

Our view: automate the movement of agreed information decisively. Make a delivery owner explicitly accept anything that commits the business to work.

What should a sales-to-operations handover contain?

A handover should carry the confirmed customer and deal IDs, final signed scope or order, products or services, commercial exclusions, delivery dates and dependencies, named contacts, agreed milestones, billing triggers and the person who owns the work. Include the authoritative signed document or approved quote version—not a paraphrased promise in CRM notes. If a term is uncertain, operations should see the gap before accepting the work.

The Olivers Consulting two-decision handover model

Commercially won does not mean operationally ready

Deal won → Handover pack checked → Delivery owner accepts → Work starts

Use two independent decisions: sales confirms the commercial agreement, then operations confirms that the work can be delivered with the agreed information and resources. Between them, automation moves data and assigns missing items, but does not quietly invent scope.

Why isn't a 'closed won' trigger enough?

A CRM stage tells you that a commercial event has occurred. It does not guarantee the latest proposal is signed, a delivery owner is available, implementation details are complete or a customer's requested start date is feasible. Trigger the preparation of a delivery record automatically, but define a separate acceptance rule for starting work. This is the difference between removing repetitive data entry and automating an unsupported commitment.

Which fields should move from CRM to the delivery tool?

Transfer only the fields operations actually needs: unique deal and customer identifiers, agreed offering and scope reference, deliverables, start constraints, primary contact, commercial owner, delivery owner, approval status and agreed dates. Preserve the source and revision of every important field. If the project tool already has a record for that deal ID, update it under an agreed rule rather than creating another project.

How do you stop duplicate projects when a deal is updated?

Use the CRM opportunity ID as an idempotency key, or map it to one persistent delivery record ID. Trigger the workflow on the transition into the won state rather than on every later edit. Handle retries by checking whether a project already exists and recording the previous outcome. When a deal is reopened or re-won, decide whether the existing project should be revised, paused or reactivated; never assume a new project is always appropriate.

What should operations check before accepting a job?

The receiving team should confirm scope, capacity, dates, client dependencies, access requirements, financial or purchasing prerequisites and responsibility for the first milestone. If any required element is absent, a targeted task should identify the gap and its owner. An automated notification is not evidence that operations has accepted the job.

How do approvals and changes fit the handover?

A material scope difference, price change, promised delivery date or customer concession may need commercial approval before the receiving team can proceed. Keep the accepted deal version linked to the project and log every approved change. Our approval workflow guide explains routing and recorded decisions.

How does this connect to quoting?

The quote automation guide addresses pricing rules, proposal creation and sign-off before a customer agrees. Handover uses the resulting approved agreement as input; it should not replace commercial validation with whatever happens to be typed in the deal description.

How is this different from client onboarding?

The client onboarding guide covers the trusted client record, system setup and initial customer journey. Sales-to-operations handover is the preceding commercial-to-delivery acceptance decision: what was sold, what must be delivered and who accepts the commitment. Both workflows can share the same client ID without duplicating data.

What can existing CRM platforms do?

You may not need new software. HubSpot's official projects guidance describes creating project records through deal-based workflows following a won stage. Microsoft's Dynamics 365 sales-process documentation describes the movement from opportunity through quote, order and fulfilment. Specific features depend on your product, licence and configuration. The missing piece is frequently the business's acceptance and exception rules.

Worked example: a signed deal missing a delivery date

Imagine a hypothetical consultancy that wins a £7,500 project. The CRM record links to a signed proposal and a named client contact, but the implementation start is recorded only as “as soon as possible”. The workflow creates a provisional delivery record, carries over the signed scope and asks sales to confirm the start constraints. Operations can prepare its checklist, but the project does not become accepted until the owner confirms a feasible date. The monetary value is illustrative, not a reported client result.

What should be automated first?

Start with one standard service category and a short handover checklist. Connect the won-deal trigger, retrieve the authoritative proposal, create or match a delivery record, identify missing fields and notify the intended owner. Add automatic task creation after the handover has been accepted. Avoid automating every possible service variation before the core data and ownership work reliably.

How should you pilot and measure the workflow?

  1. Choose a small number of recently won deals and identify the actual delivery records.
  2. Agree the handover fields with sales, finance and operations.
  3. Define the signed scope and the person authorised to accept delivery.
  4. Run the automation without creating live customer-facing commitments.
  5. Test missing dates, changed scope, duplicate triggers and a reopened opportunity.
  6. Compare every generated record with the approved deal evidence.
  7. Enable the internal handover only when exceptions have clear owners.

Measure missing handover information, duplicate project records, time from deal won to operational acceptance and rework caused by incorrect promises. An instant project-creation timestamp is not proof that the handover succeeded.

When is additional integration worthwhile?

If your CRM and project system are already linked, improve the rules before buying another platform. A targeted integration is worthwhile where staff repeatedly retype agreed scope, deliverables and contacts, or where the commercial and delivery records drift apart. Questions about sales-to-operations handover

Can a closed-won CRM deal trigger an operations project automatically?

Yes, if the CRM and project system support the relevant workflow. Create or link a delivery record from a confirmed deal, then check the agreed scope, owner and start conditions before marking the handover accepted.

What should happen when handover information is missing?

The workflow should identify the missing field, assign it to the salesperson or commercial owner and keep the delivery record in a not-ready state until resolved.

Is sales handover the same as client onboarding?

No. Sales handover transfers what the business promised and who owns delivery. Client onboarding then handles customer setup, access, documents and the relationship's practical start. They should share a trusted client and deal reference.
